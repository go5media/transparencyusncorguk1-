Hosting a community camera

The benefits, responsibilities and safeguards of hosting a USCN community camera.

What does a camera host receive?

Hosting a USCN community camera is a practical exchange, not simply a request for free electricity and internet. Where a site is approved, USCN supplies and installs its own camera and normally provides routine maintenance, configuration, security updates and eventual removal without a direct equipment or routine service charge to the host. The host contributes an agreed location, power, suitable connectivity and reasonable access. Exact responsibilities are defined in the signed hosting agreement.

A visible security presence at your premises

A properly positioned, clearly signed camera may deter some incidents and can provide useful evidence of activity within its approved field of view, which may include an appropriate part of the host’s frontage. Camera coverage is designed around an evidenced community-safety need and the rights of people nearby. USCN cannot promise that crime will be prevented, that all areas of the property will be covered or that any particular incident will be recorded.

USCN manages requests about its camera

For recordings controlled by USCN, our trained team deals with the footage search, relevant preservation decisions, assessment of data-subject rights requests and lawful disclosures, including appropriate privacy protections for third parties. This can remove substantial administrative work for a participating shop or other host. People can approach USCN directly. If a request about the USCN camera comes to the host, the host should forward it promptly to data.protection@datahandling.uscn.org.uk, with no unnecessary personal information or independent footage handling.

USCN's controller role concerns its own network cameras. Hosting does not remove legal obligations arising from a host's own CCTV, employment activities, independent use of images or any separately determined processing purposes.

Incident assistance without searching recordings yourself

If something occurs near the premises, tell our team promptly where and when it happened and what you are concerned about. We can identify the relevant camera and assess whether footage exists, whether it can still be preserved and how it can lawfully be used. Where justified, USCN searches and secures relevant footage, assesses any third-party privacy interests and considers disclosure to an entitled requester or to law enforcement.

Our internal aim for routine, sufficiently specified incident-footage enquiries is to complete the assessment and, where lawful, provide an outcome within seven calendar days. This is an operational target, not a guaranteed delivery period, and complex cases may take longer. Statutory requests follow their own legal deadlines. Contact us as soon as possible: routine recordings are ordinarily overwritten within a maximum of 31 days, and footage may already be unavailable. No request automatically preserves or entitles someone to receive video.

Complimentary advertising at the host location

Under the applicable agreed host and signage terms, a qualifying host may receive the complimentary Right (Economy) advertising position on eligible signage at their own hosted camera location only—not advertising throughout the town. The equivalent current commercial advertising-position rate is £20 per sign. Any associated QR redirect is governed by separate eligibility and fair-use terms. This does not give the host access to CCTV data.

How is the service funded?

USCN supports community-camera operations through legitimate commercial CCTV work and paid physical signage advertising. Advertisers buy prepaid placements on physical signs, not footage, camera control, personal data or decisions about where surveillance is necessary. USCN may also benefit from public awareness of its workmanship and services. Community-camera deployment remains needs-led, lawful and proportionate, irrespective of promotional or commercial opportunities.

Can hosts watch their camera live?

Not automatically. A separate, restricted business-host live-viewing pilot may be considered following a camera-specific assessment and written approval. It permits a narrowly authorised live view only, without playback, export or administration. Residential access may be considered after the business pilot has been evaluated and any further approvals are obtained.

Payment and joining

There is currently no general payment commitment to hosts. A possible future remuneration programme is described as a proposal in USCN's strategic roadmap; it is not an active entitlement. Hosting is voluntary and subject to assessment, written terms and camera-specific operational approval.

To discuss hosting or report a camera issue, use our contact page. For data rights or incident-footage requests, contact the data protection team.
